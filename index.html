Component research

Open up the classroom tower and measure each part with a ruler or calipers, then check the manufacturer's spec sheet. A part is marked done once it has its size, how it mounts, how it stays cool, and what cables it needs.

Quick converter

Spec sheets usually list millimeters. Keep all your measurements in inches so the layout planner works.

Case materials

Pick what your case will be made from. It can't be a store-bought computer case. It must be safe around electricity and heat, and strong enough to hold everything.

Case design

Set your case size, drag your parts into place, and mark where air comes in and goes out. This is a side view, like looking through a glass side panel, with the front of the case on the left.

Case dimensions

Measure the box your design fits inside, even if your case is round or oddly shaped.

A typical mid-tower is about 18 in long, 17 in tall and 8 in wide. Smaller is fine if everything fits and can still breathe.

Layout planner

Drag a part from the list onto the case, or tap Place. Then drag it inside the case to move it. Tap an airflow arrow to turn it. Parts you haven't measured yet use a typical size and show a dashed outline until you enter your real measurements.

Fit check

    Airflow plan

    Describe the path air takes through your case. Cool air should come in low and front, hot air should leave high and back.

    Access for upgrades and repairs

    How will someone get inside to swap RAM, add a drive or clean out dust?

    Detailed sketch or 3D model

    Upload a photo of your labeled sketch, or link to your 3D model (Tinkercad, SketchUp, Onshape). Label the case dimensions, part locations, vents and fans, and openings.

    Research report

    Summarize your research in about one page (roughly 300–550 words). Use the buttons to pull in what you've already written, then rewrite it into full sentences.

    Component sizes and requirements

    Which parts take up the most space or need the most care? What sizes drove your design?

    Chosen materials and why they're suitable

    Explain strength, safety (conductivity and heat), and how you'll work with it.

    Potential challenges and your plan

    Name at least three problems you expect, and how you'll handle each. Think heat, weight, mounting the motherboard, cable routing and the power button.

    Presentation plan

    Plan a 5–7 minute presentation to the class. Give each part of your talk a time, write your talking points, then rehearse with the timer.
